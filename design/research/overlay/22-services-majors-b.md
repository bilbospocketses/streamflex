**Browser targets (revision 3, user decision):** Windows Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Linux Chrome, Edge, Firefox (full non-snap). Region US.

**Linux platform facts that apply to every DRM service below (read once, referenced per service).**
- **Widevine on Linux is software-only (L3), whatever the browser.** "Linux users are essentially stuck at the L3 level ... regardless of what browser they use", so DRM services give "standard definition (SD), or, best case scenario, ... 720p", and no HDR (https://www.xda-developers.com/linux-finally-working-hdr-but-still-cant-use-it-most-streaming-services/, dated 2026-06-04; REPORTED, one secondary source, but the claim is technically consistent). Treat "720p max, SDR, stereo" as the planning number for Linux Chrome and Firefox on any DRM service unless a service-specific source says otherwise.
- **Edge on Linux has no Widevine by default.** Microsoft's own Q&A answer: "Unfortunately, Widevine DRM is not available on Linux now"; the flag `edge://flags/#edge-widevine-drm` shows "Not available on your platform ... Mac, Windows". A possible workaround is launching with `--enable-features=msWidevinePlatform` (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/2241881/widevine-drm-disabled-for-linux-environments; VERIFIED for the flag text and the answer, undated; the workaround is unconfirmed). So **Linux Edge cannot play any Widevine-DRM service out of the box**; only non-DRM content (most of YouTube) works. Edge on Linux is also not PlayReady-capable, so there is no other DRM path.
- Per-service "official browser list names or omits Linux" is given in each section.
